# Editor selection

Proceed as the offer article: lead with the operational loop, close with the offer to run it. The angle is not "here is a case study" and not "here is a research recap" -- it's "here is the whole method, in the order you'd actually build it, and Anthus already runs this." The reader should be able to describe the loop back after reading, and should also know exactly who to call.

Why this angle: the idea brief and assignment both call this the top of the client-acquisition funnel, sitting above the Kanbus and Papyrus pieces, all landing on the same engagement page. An article that reads as pure research recap undersells the offer; one that reads as pure sales copy without the mechanism undersells the credibility the distillation and confidence work already earned. Giving away the whole method is the credibility move -- a buyer who could not get this level of detail from a vendor that hadn't actually built it.

Structure locked from the report: scenario first (no product name), then the six-step loop introducing vocabulary as needed, then the month-one/month-six scorecard framed explicitly as a composite shape of the method rather than one client's literal timeline, then controlled rollout (per-slice gate, the cascade test that didn't help and was dropped), then Call Criteria for scale in duration/cumulative terms only, then the close.

Keep: the honest caveat that the student-beats-teacher result is corpus-specific: it demonstrates the mechanism, not a promised outcome. Keep the cascade-didn't-help finding as evidence of rigor. Keep Call Criteria strictly to years-in-production and cumulative counts, no present-tense operational claim.

Drop: the label-source comparison's full experimental detail (direct fine-tune vs. distilled, same 140 labels) -- the finding that fine-tuning alone without eight points of distillation "the teacher's information" leaves accuracy behind is useful as one sentence, not a subsection; the sibling article already carries the derivation and can be linked if useful, but it isn't required by the assignment and would crowd the loop-first structure.

Cap the "X, not Y" contrast at one use: "a classifier that gets smarter, not one that just runs" is the strongest candidate and should anchor the close, not the lead, so it lands as the takeaway.

CTA: one link to `/engage`, placed at the close, after the method has been given away in full.
