# Editor selection

**2026-09-22.** Split from the flagship, "We Told the AI She Was a Woman. It Demoted Her."
(ANTH-3cb9808e-1dd7-4834-881f-c6fb0fdbec1b). The flagship becomes a roughly 1,200-word
general-reader story built around one composite example (the paralegal/attorney pair) with a
labelled composite illustration marking it as constructed. This piece and its sibling, "Can You
Fix It? Gating, Averaging and Fine-Tuning Against a Gendered Verdict," carry the full engineering
treatment the flagship no longer has room for.

Proceed with the full-measurement angle: one method, three demographic axes (gender, race, age),
two engines held to the identical test, reported side by side with intervals and floors, and the
pre-registered predictions that held next to the ones that didn't. This is the piece for a reader
who wants the method well enough to run it on their own decision. The client (Ryan) asked for the
split and approved this piece's scope; final editorial approval is his.

Why this angle: the flagship's job is now to make one reader feel the paralegal/attorney result;
this piece's job is to give the reader who wants more the entire measurement, not a single
example. Splitting them lets each piece be honest about what it's for, instead of asking one
story to be both an entry point and a reference.

Keep both engines under the same test and in the same table throughout. Lead the race section
with the inconclusive first attempt and the reversed direction, unexplained -- do not speculate
on why. Keep the "old problem, three new shapes" framing as the closing context, and end by
pointing the reader who asks "can this be fixed?" to the sibling piece rather than answering here.
