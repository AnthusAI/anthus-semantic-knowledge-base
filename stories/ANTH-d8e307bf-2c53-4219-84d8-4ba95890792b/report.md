# Editorial report

## Central finding

Chatticus should not be introduced as another agent that keeps working after a
browser tab closes. That benefit is real but generic. The product's distinctive
claim is control over the organization: where it runs, which models perform
which jobs, what they cost, what private systems they can reach, who reviews
their work, and which actions stop for a person.

This position is legible because two neighboring products occupy opposite ends
of the tradeoff. Grok Bot supplies a polished bot roster and cloud computer
inside a vendor service. OpenClaw supplies an open platform that runs where the
user chooses, while leaving the user to supply, secure, and operate the host.
Chatticus is designed to productize the organization inside infrastructure the
customer controls.

## Argument

The launch article should make three connected claims.

1. **Agent work is becoming organizational.** Once several persistent agents
   can assign, review, reject, and return work, the system's reporting lines and
   acceptance procedures matter as much as any individual prompt.
2. **Model choice is operating policy.** Useful capability is becoming cheaper
   quickly. A company should be able to assign value models to routine work,
   reserve premium models for difficult or consequential work, and measure the
   all-in cost of outcomes it accepts.
3. **Infrastructure placement is a control boundary.** The same decision that
   determines who operates the computer also determines which private systems,
   credentials, and data the agents can reach. Chatticus puts that decision
   with the customer.

## Selected structure

1. Open with the emerging category's control-versus-convenience tradeoff.
2. Name the missing quadrant and introduce Chatticus.
3. Describe the agent organization in plain language.
4. Explain models and budgets as role-specific policy.
5. Explain customer AWS and local workers as the secure integration boundary.
6. Explain approvals, durable records, and the continued human responsibility.
7. State the build status honestly and close on the compact promise:
   “Your agents. Your models. Your cloud.”

## Voice

Write as a product announcement grounded in firsthand observations, not a
manifesto and not a competitive attack. The confidence should come from making
the tradeoffs explicit. Prefer concrete operational verbs—assign, review,
reject, route, connect, approve—to abstractions such as orchestration and
governance.

## Evidence boundary

- Describe competitor capabilities only where official documentation supports
  them.
- Phrase Grok Bot model control as “no per-bot model choice is documented,” not
  an absolute statement about its internals.
- Phrase Chatticus multi-model routing, private customer deployment, and secure
  custom integration as intended design until they ship.
- State that OpenAI is the current provider and that additional providers are
  future work.
- Do not use current pricing figures that may go stale before launch.

## Publication decision

Select for copywriting now. Do not advance to `published` and do not change the
site MDX state from `unpublished` until the Chatticus launch decision.
