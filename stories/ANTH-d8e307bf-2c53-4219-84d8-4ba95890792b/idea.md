# Idea

## Working title

Chatticus: The AI Organization You Control

## Pitch

Grok Bot proved that named, persistent bots on a shared computer can feel like
an organization rather than a pile of chat sessions. OpenClaw proved that
people want the other side of the equation: their infrastructure, their keys,
their data, and their choice of models. The tradeoff today is that one product
owns the machine and the model, while the other leaves the user operating and
securing a laptop, homelab, or VPS.

Chatticus occupies the missing quadrant. It is a productized organization of
AI teammates designed to run in the customer's AWS account or on attached local
hardware. The customer chooses models and settings by role or task class,
defines which private systems the organization can reach, and keeps
consequential actions behind explicit approval gates.

The article introduces Chatticus through the control problem, not a feature
tour. Model choice is a cost and governance policy. Infrastructure placement is
an access boundary. Reporting lines, review procedures, and human gates are
system design.

## Why now

The product concept follows directly from four recent Anth.us articles:

- Grok Bot Gave My Coding Agents a Boss
- From pair programmer to executive
- The Year Coding Became a Commodity
- Maximize Value, Not Intelligence

Together they describe the category, the organizational shift, and the model
economics. Chatticus is the concrete product response.

## Audience

Technical founders, AI engineering leaders, security-conscious operators, and
small teams that want persistent agent organizations without surrendering model,
cost, infrastructure, or access control.

## Visual

A wide editorial cutaway: the human visibly owns the boundary and sets
direction from above; one manager agent coordinates several worker agents below;
private infrastructure and an approval gate remain inside the same environment.
