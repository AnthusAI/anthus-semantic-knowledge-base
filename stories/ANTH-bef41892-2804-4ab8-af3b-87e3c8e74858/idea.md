# Idea

## Pitch

A new class of work just became automatable: standing up a whole product overnight with loops and a hierarchical subagent swarm. The frontier in The Year Coding Became a Commodity is still moving. This is the next rung. Price and autonomy multiplied until you could delegate whole milestones. Now you can ship the product.

We already did it twice. B0RD and Chattic.us both started that way.

Then every solution makes a new problem. The leftover gates are QA, privacy, and security. They cannot stay on a human-critical path at that speed. This is also a sibling of Bugonomics: the flip side of cheap coding is not only cheap offense. It is that we cannot inspect our own output as fast as we can produce it.


## Chattic.us beat (Ryan, 2026-08-31)

First-person receipt. Do not invent around it.

Extensive steering in advance with expensive models (Opus, Sol). During the actual development, Ryan's only real contribution was to log into AWS occasionally and set some nameservers to launch the website. Pretty much everything else, the swarm did.

The point to make: there is a shift into a management role where all you really need to do is provide authorization. The work is already delegated. It happens by itself. You know you are succeeding because your only contribution is to log into stuff, and things proceed anyway while you lean back. That is amazing, and it is the overnight-products story, not a metaphor.

The leftover human job in that night was authorization (AWS, DNS), not writing the product. Do not turn this into the pair-programmer-to-executive article. That piece is the org chart. This is the night the product shipped.

Outside specimen, not the hero: Steve Yegge, The Continuous Thunderdome (August 2026). After Gas Town, he bonded a bespoke harness to a real application. Human review came off the path. The merge queue physically broke, then he megabatch-landed. Same leftover: the old craft gates cannot keep up.

## Audience

People who just used loops and swarms to ship, and anyone watching QA, privacy, and security fall behind the new pace.
