# All I do now is log in

## Thesis

The approval gate gets all the attention: people write that their agents do nearly everything and that they only type “approved,” because approval feels like judgment and judgment feels dignified. Almost nobody writes about the authentication gate. Typing a password for a robot feels like being the help. Yet authentication is the gate that genuinely cannot be automated away: it exists because somebody built a control that demands a human body, and every agent product ends up with a hole shaped exactly like it.

The confident version of that sentence has not been written yet: “all I do now is log in” as proof that the setup works, rather than as a confession of risk or a tedious exception. Peter Yang’s 2026-09-02 Creator Economy piece is the same hole seen from the fearful side: he refuses to type a Google password into a cloud browser. This piece asks what the positive version would mean.

## Research finding

The exact public framing was not found. No first-person author located says that their remaining human role is specifically authentication, credential entry, 2FA, CAPTCHA, or permission approval and explicitly presents that login-only role as evidence that their automation is working well. The public sentiment exists only cut in half: there are enthusiastic accounts of agents doing nearly everything while the human approves or judges at the end, and separate discussions of humans handling login, 2FA, or credentials. There is no located source that combines both halves with the requested positive “this proves my setup is dialed in” framing.
