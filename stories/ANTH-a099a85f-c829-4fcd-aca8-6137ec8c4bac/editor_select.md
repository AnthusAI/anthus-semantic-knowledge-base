# Editor selection

Proceed with the how-to angle: answer "can you fine-tune Jev" with a flat no in the first two sentences, sourced to TypeSafe's own model page, then spend the piece on what you can change and what we measured when we changed it. The spine is the natural experiment in the recorded run (87 labels of refitting against one new question), because it explains in one table why the two levers have different jobs.

Chosen over two alternatives. A pure results piece would bury the mechanism a Jev user needs in order to copy it. A "layer versus fine-tuning" showdown would promise a win the data doesn't give: on this corpus gradient fine-tuning an open engine beats the layer on raw accuracy with the same labels. That comparison stays in, as one section, told against our own wrong pre-registered predictions, and the detail goes to the Jev vs Laya piece.

Conditions: the 3-of-12 reliability result gets its own section and chart, not a caveat; the labeler is called a script every time it matters; the calibration section reconciles with "Can You Trust Jev's Confidence?" only as far as the code supports, and leaves the rest open; vendor statements are attributed to TypeSafe and Convai; results still running are marked pending and filled before publication.

The client, Ryan Porter, asked for this piece as one of three in the Jev series and approved the angles in the series brief. Final editorial approval is his.
